\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The procedural corpus is legal and reproducible but represents one designed distribution rather than the breadth of contemporary compositional practice. A model can learn the generator's regularities without transferring to independent human-authored scores. Validation on legally supplied MusicXML and blind ratings by composers or post-tonal analysts remain necessary.

Automatic metrics reduce complex musical judgments to inspectable features. Pc-set coverage is recall-like, interval vectors ignore ordering and register, cyclic row accuracy depends on serialized note order, and gesture consistency uses hand-specified feature targets. Strict serial-form accuracy returns zero when a fragment contains no complete aggregate, which distinguishes prefix agreement from complete transformation realization but can penalize intentionally sparse sketches.

The event vocabulary uses a 4/4 sixteenth-note grid and omits tuplets, microtonality, changing meters, extended techniques, detailed articulation, beaming, dynamics in the evaluated configuration, and engraving layout. One instrument label is repeated across parts, so mixed-ensemble orchestration is outside the current interface. Structural MusicXML validity and requested part/measure counts do not establish publication-quality notation.

Condition-preserving windows expose every saved body token but restrict each prediction to a 256-token context. Candidate reranking uses a fixed candidate budget and penalty vector, and most reported constraint metrics overlap with that selection objective. Sensitivity to weights, sampling temperature, candidate count, and alternative symbolic encodings remains to be tested.

Finally, corrected full neural metrics are pending. Smoke outputs and legacy v2 experiments are retained only for engineering history and must not be interpreted as final evidence.
