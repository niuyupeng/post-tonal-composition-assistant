\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The corrected design isolates three questions that were confounded in the legacy workflow. First, serial examples and small-set examples now have mutually compatible pitch targets. Second, the single-candidate and guided decoders share one trained generator, so their comparison concerns inference-time selection rather than initialization differences. Third, condition ablations preserve corpus membership and target events while hiding only selected prefix fields.

The revised metric protocol also separates optimization targets from held-out diagnostics. Candidate reranking can use a deterministic rhythmic-profile template, but it cannot inspect the stochastic density curve realized in the corpus target. Pc-set precision supplements recall-like coverage, strict serial-form accuracy is distinct from cyclic row order, and requested score span and voice count are measured explicitly.

These changes improve interpretability but do not establish artistic value. The corpus remains procedural, gesture consistency remains heuristic, and several automatic metrics also participate in candidate selection. Any favorable full-run change will therefore concern implemented symbolic controllability on this testbed. Composer preference, material coherence, engraving usefulness, and transfer to independent scores require blind expert ratings and legally supplied external MusicXML.

No corrected neural result is interpreted here before the formal run completes. Once the v3 CSV and JSON artifacts exist, this section should discuss endpoint-specific gains and trade-offs, not claim uniform superiority.
